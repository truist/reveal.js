## Reflections (1/2)

We went to Thailand in August, which is "monsoon season", i.e. the off-season, i.e. it's crazy to go to Thailand in August.

("Monsoon" refers to the wind, but it's usually also very rainy.)

We planned our trip to skip some places because they would be too wet and muddy.
But still, I fretted a lot about whether the trip was just going to be endless storms and wind.

**Nope, not at all.** It rained for an hour or so, every other day or so.

---

<!-- .slide: class="only-text" -->
## Reflections (2/2)

And August has some huge upsides!

No tourists! (We had the cave all to ourselves!)  
Fruit is all in season!  
Everything is green!

And in January to March, especially in the north,  
farmers burn their crops, and smoke is a huge issue.

August. It's the perfect time to go to Thailand. Really.

### Reflections

Bangkok is a big city. Great if you want the opportunity and intensity and efficiency. Not so great if you want peace and quiet. But it was fun, for a day 😁

It's impressive how consistently all the Thai people wear masks, inside and out. It's certainly not 100% of the people 100% of the time, but the default is "mask on". Even in the consistent 30° heat (that's 85° for Americans) and high humidity. It puts Americans to shame. (And Thailand has a higher COVID vaccination rate!)

## Reflections (1/3)

The choice of which island to visit was complicated.

First, monsoon season really does make terrible weather for the famous tourist islands off the west coast.
But a few islands off the east coast have perfect weather in August - as we saw!

The biggest and most-touristy of those islands is Koh Samui.
(It's where you'll find the Ritz Carlton!)
So we avoided it 😉

The next-biggest, and most-Thai-like, is Koh Phangan.  
And it's close to the famous scuba site Sail Rock. 🎉

But it's also home to the (in)famous [Full Moon Party](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Full_Moon_Party) 😱  

But our schedule meant we missed it! 🎉

---

<!-- .slide: class="only-text" -->
## Reflections (2/3)

Both islands are tourist traps.  
For example, taxi rides are (over-)priced throughout the islands,  
per-person rather than per-trip, unlike the rest of Thailand.

But covid closed quite a few businesses, and many seemed under-maintained.
We learned that the scuba industry suffered greatly, too.

That's a part of what led us to switch hotels after the first night.
The one we were in was clearly once very nice... but now it seemed under-maintained and under-staffed.
And the location was gorgeous, but hard to get to, and not convenient to nice beaches.

---

<!-- .slide: class="only-text" -->
## Reflections (3/3)

Through visiting places, switching hotels, and scuba diving, we explored nearly every part of Koh Phangan.
We didn't spend much time in the main town (where the ferry dock is), and we avoided the particular beach where the Full Moon party is held.

But we learned that different parts of the island each have their own "feel".
The area we started in was tourist-oriented, and focused on 20-somethings trying to have fun and live cheap.
The area we ended up in was quiet, and upscale, and very out-of-the-way.
If we go back, we'll definitely go there again.

It was nice to be so far away from civilization 😁

## Reflections

Scuba diving is incredible!

But no picture can ever do it justice.  
So I don't really mind not having taken any.

I'll try to convey the feeling, though...

---

Imagine being calm and feeling weightless, coasting slowly along next to a wall of coral that extends 15 meters down and 15 more up, with fish darting in and out everywhere.

Then roll over onto your back and just lay there, looking up.

Weightless.

See the rays of sunlight streaming down from the surface, and thousands of little blue fish swimming all around you, close enough to touch.
Then see your 12-year-old son drifting by above you, and wave to him, and he waves back.

If you can relate to that, you can get some sense of what it's like 🤩

## Final reflections: baggage

I was very proud of how we packed.
Each person had a 40L backpack, plus we had three "personal item" bags (between the four of us), plus the checked bag with the scuba gear.
We bought one more "personal item" bag while we were there, for souvenirs.

If we hadn't brought the scuba gear,

I definitely recommend this way of packing, for any Thailand trip.
(And if you want to borrow a bag, we have some great ones!)

## Final reflections: money

It's very expensive to get there from here.

But once you're there, it's very possible to live very cheaply.
The YouTube videos about "Thailand for $20/day" (per person) aren't lying.
But we spent far more than that, by choice - we did a lot of things, and enjoyed a lot of luxuries, and it was totally worth it 😁

On the other hand, cash, *cash*, **cash**.
We did not fully appreciate how *everything* requires cash.
I think the only places we used credit cards were for the transportation tickets we bought online, and the one 5-star hotel in downtown Bangkok.

**Every single other thing was cash.**

## Final reflections: overall

Boy was it worth it!

My favorites, in order: Nan De Panna, elephant camp, scuba diving, the cave, authentic Thai food, night markets, the crazy way they drive, night train.

I could live without: cities, temples, touristy places.

I would change: absolutely nothing 😁
